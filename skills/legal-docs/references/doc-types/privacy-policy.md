# Privacy policy (Datenschutzerklärung / privacy notice)

Checklist items point to sources. **Fetch and read every provision before you rely on it.** The
item text is a reminder of what to look for, not a statement of the law.

## When is it required

- **EU/EEA**: whenever personal data is collected from people (EU-GDPR Art. 13) or obtained from
  others (Art. 14), and the GDPR applies (Art. 3: EU establishment, or targeting/monitoring people
  in the EU; see EU-EDPB-TERRITORIAL). Practically every website (server logs contain IP addresses).
- **UK**: same under UK-GDPR Art. 13/14 for UK establishment or UK targeting.
- **CH**: CH-DSG Art. 19 (duty to inform when collecting), Art. 3 (effects in Switzerland).
- **US**: US-CA-CALOPPA for any commercial website collecting PII from California residents (no
  threshold); state comprehensive laws above their thresholds (us-states.md); US-COPPA for
  child-directed services; US-WA-MHMDA for consumer health data. US-FTC-ACT-5 makes the policy's
  promises enforceable.

## Structure (recommended)

1. Who we are (controller) and how to contact us; DPO; EU/UK/CH representative
2. Overview (layered summary, EU-EDPB-TRANSPARENCY)
3. Processing activities: one subsection per activity from the inventory record
   (website hosting & logs, account, orders & payment, contact/support, newsletter, analytics,
   marketing, AI features, social login, embedded content, …). For each: data, purpose, legal
   basis, legitimate interest if used, recipients, retention, whether mandatory
4. Recipients and processors (by category or named; named is more transparent)
5. International transfers and safeguards
6. Cookies and similar technologies (or link to the cookie policy)
7. Retention (if not per activity)
8. Your rights (+ how to exercise them) and the right to object (prominent, separate)
9. Right to withdraw consent
10. Complaint to a supervisory authority (name the competent one)
11. Automated decision-making / profiling (or a statement that none takes place, if true)
12. Children
13. Jurisdiction-specific sections (California and other US states, Switzerland, UK)
14. Changes to this policy; effective date / last updated

## Pitfalls

- Listing legal bases generically ("we process data under Art. 6 GDPR") instead of per purpose.
- Claiming "we never share data" while using processors; claiming consent while trackers load
  before the banner (inventory gaps!).
- Copying the GDPR text for UK or Swiss users without adapting it.
- Naming the BfDI as the competent authority for a private company in Germany (usually the state DPA).
- Forgetting server logs, hosting provider, e-mail provider, error tracking, fonts/CDNs.
- Linking the old EU ODR platform (repealed, EU-ODR-REPEAL), which belongs nowhere anyway.
