# Terms (Terms of Service / Terms and Conditions / AGB / CGV)

Checklist items point to sources. **Fetch and read every provision before you rely on it.**
Terms are where AI drafts do the most damage: an invalid clause in consumer terms can be void,
can be attacked by competitors or consumer associations (DE/AT), and ambiguity is construed
against the drafter (EU-UCTD Art. 5, DE-BGB-AGB § 305c(2)). Keep terms short and only include
clauses with a clear purpose that the defense report can justify.

## When is it required

Not always legally mandatory as a document, but needed whenever there is a contract with users
(accounts, paid plans, SaaS, marketplace, API access), and **mandatory information** has to be
given somewhere for distance contracts with consumers (EU-CRD Art. 6; DE-EGBGB-246A; AT-FAGG § 4;
UK-CCR2013 Sch. 2). Services hosting user content must describe moderation in their terms
(EU-DSA Art. 14; UK-OSA). Decide B2C vs. B2B first (records): the rules differ fundamentally.

## Structure (recommended)

1. Scope, parties, definitions (who contracts with whom: the entity from the imprint record)
2. Conclusion of contract (steps, order button, confirmation, correction of input errors, storage of the contract text, languages)
3. Services / description of the product (and changes to digital products)
4. Prices, payment, (auto-)renewal, trials
5. Term and termination (cancellation button for consumers in DE)
6. Right of withdrawal (consumers) → reference to the withdrawal notice
7. User obligations, acceptable use, user content licence
8. Content moderation, notice and action, complaints (if hosting user content)
9. Warranty / conformity (consumers: statutory rights, cannot be excluded)
10. Liability
11. Intellectual property / open source notices
12. Data protection → reference to the privacy policy (do not put consent into terms)
13. Changes to the terms (B2C: limited)
14. Dispute resolution information, governing law, jurisdiction
15. Accessibility information (consumer e-commerce services, unless micro-enterprise)
16. Final provisions (severability only where useful; avoid boilerplate that is invalid in B2C)

## Pitfalls

- Using B2B boilerplate (broad liability caps, unilateral changes, "as is") in consumer terms.
- Choice-of-law clauses that suggest consumers lose their home-country protection (check
  Rome I Art. 6 with a lawyer; it is not bundled).
- Unilateral change clauses ("we may change these terms at any time") in B2C.
- Copying US-style ALL-CAPS disclaimers into EU consumer terms.
- Putting data protection consent into the terms.
- Forgetting that the withdrawal function, order button and cancellation button are UI duties.
